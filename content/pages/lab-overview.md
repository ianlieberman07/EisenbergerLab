title: Lab Overview
---

Research in the Social and Affective Neuroscience Laboratory aims to understand why social relationships matter so profoundly for both mental and physical health, and to identify the neural mechanisms that make this so. Our work is organized around three broad questions: What are the neural underpinnings of "social pain"—the hurt that follows rejection, exclusion, or loss? What are the neural underpinnings of social connection—the warmth and safety we feel in the presence of close others? And can understanding these processes help explain the well-documented links between our social ties and our health? Using a combination of neuroimaging, pharmacological, physiological, and behavioral methods, we trace the pathways that run in both directions between the social world and the body: how social experiences get under the skin to shape stress and immune responses, and how bodily states like inflammation reshape social experience in turn. Across this work, a consistent theme emerges—that the presence of close others, and our efforts to care for them, are not peripheral comforts but core regulators of how we respond to threat, pain, and stress.
