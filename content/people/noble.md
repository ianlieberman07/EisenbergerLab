Russell Noble received his BA in Cognitive Science with a focus in decision and cognition from the University of Michigan. Russell's current research uses social and affective neuroscience and pharmacological techniques to study the neurobiological basis of prosocial behavior. Currently, he is interested in how the brain manages personal distress in emotionally charged helping contexts and why caregiving and prosocial behavior can produce distinctive affective consequences, such as feelings of warmth, affiliation, and stress buffering. While at UCLA he has also studied the neural development during adolescence that supports prosocial behavior. In his spare time Russell enjoys cooking, golfing, going to farmer's markets, and thrift stores.
